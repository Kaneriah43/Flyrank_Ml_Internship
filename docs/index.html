Content Refresh Opportunity Scoring

Harsh · MSc Advanced Computer Science · University of Liverpool · FlyRank ML Internship Capstone · September 2026

Abstract This paper investigates which content pages in a real anonymized search dataset show observable signals of underperformance relative to their position tier. Using 176,738 page-month records from the FlyRank internship warehouse, a Random Forest classifier was trained to score pages by refresh priority using CTR, impressions, position, and content age as features. The model achieved Precision@50 of 1.000 on a stratified held-out split, compared to 0.640 for a hand-written rule baseline. All results are directional and decision-support only — the output is a ranked editorial queue, not a guarantee of ranking recovery.

1. Introduction

Content teams managing large websites face a prioritisation problem: hundreds of pages compete for limited editorial hours each week. Without a systematic scoring method, teams rely on gut feel or simple rules — "flag anything not updated in 90 days" — that ignore the interaction between content age, visibility, and click-through performance relative to search position.

This paper builds a repeatable scoring model that ranks pages by observed underperformance signals, producing a weekly priority queue a content strategist can act on directly. The output is decision-support, not automation — a human reviewer checks every high-priority candidate before any editorial action is taken.

Decision supported: Which pages should a content team prioritise for review this week, given limited editorial hours?

What was excluded and why

  • Pages with gsc_data_available = FALSE — no reliable signal to train on
  • Final month (2026-06) — treated as sealed test month to prevent label leakage; using it for label development would let future outcomes contaminate training
  • All client identifiers hashed — no client names, domains, or URLs appear anywhere in this paper or repo

3. Methodology

Label definition (proxy)

No ground-truth refresh outcome exists in the data — there is no column confirming whether a refresh improved performance. A proxy label is constructed: a page is flagged as a priority candidate (is_declining_label = 1) when it has above-median impressions AND below-median CTR for its position tier. This identifies pages Google is still showing to users but that are underperforming on clicks relative to similar-ranked pages.

Honest caveat: This proxy is an assumption, not a confirmed outcome. A page matching this definition is a candidate for review — not a guaranteed case for refresh.

Baseline

Hand rule — flag pages that are stale (≥90 days since update), visible (above-median impressions), and underperforming CTR at their position tier. Ranked by impressions to break ties.

Model

Random Forest classifier: 100 trees, max_depth=5, class_weight=balanced. Starting with a Decision Tree (max_depth=3) as a readable stepping stone. Both compared against the hand rule on the same split and same metric.

Validation design

Stratified 80/20 train/test split (random_state=42). Grouped split by client was attempted but failed — positive labels too sparse (1.9% positive rate) to survive client-level partitioning. Stratified split used as honest alternative, guaranteeing both classes appear in train and test.

Leakage check

All features confirmed knowable at decision time. Label column excluded from feature set. No future-window columns used. No product flags or client-derived labels introduced.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • Proxy label only — no confirmed refresh outcome exists; the label is a constructed signal, not ground truth
  • One month of data — patterns may not generalise across seasons or content strategy shifts
  • P@50 = 1.000 caveat — strong result at 1.9% positive rate, but reflects a clean proxy signal, not confirmed real-world impact
  • Correlation, not causation — no claim is made that refresh actions cause ranking recovery
  • Client-specific — model should be retrained before applying to a different domain or content strategy
  • Stratified split limitation — grouped split by client was attempted but failed due to label sparsity; results may overestimate cross-client generalisability
  • All findings are observed, measured, directional, and decision-support only

6. Ranked Recommendations

Every page receives a priority score and is assigned to one of three action tiers. A human reviewer checks every REVIEW_NOW page before acting — the score surfaces candidates, not decisions.

Tier Reason code Recommended action Human check required
REVIEW_NOW high_impressions_low_ctr_for_position Prioritise for title, meta, or content review this week Is topic still relevant? Is position realistic?
MONITOR moderate_signal_watch_next_month Flag for review next cycle if signal persists Check again after next data refresh
HOLD no_action_needed No editorial action needed now None required

What must never be automated

  • Publishing content changes based on model score alone
  • Deleting or redirecting low-priority pages
  • Sending client-facing recommendations without human sign-off
  • Making causal claims about refresh impact from model scores

Monitoring & retrain triggers

  • Precision@50 drops below 0.5 on a fresh month's data
  • Positive rate drifts more than 50% from baseline (1.9%)
  • More than 3 months since last retrain
